THE RULES - CLAUSE I. LOW AND TOLERABLE INFRACTIONS


The classification of these infractions as "tolerable" does not imply administrative permission, endorsement, or indifference. It signifies that these offenses are recognized as lower-tier disruptions that do not immediately threaten the structural security of the infrastructure.

However, these actions are strictly monitored. While they may not warrant summary banishment upon a single occurrence, their cumulative impact destabilizes the community’s standard of decorum. Repeated, persistent, or bad-faith execution of these offenses will result in an immediate upgrade to high-severity infractions, bypassing all standard leniency.

In order to prevent semantic manipulation or the feigning of ignorance, the administration officially defines and expounds upon the parameters of minor structural misconduct:


ARTICLE I. SPAM AND SYSTEMATIC FLOODING
a. The rapid, redundant, or unnecessary transmission of text, media, emojis, or graphical reactions designed to clutter the communication ledger.

b. This behavior artificially inflates traffic, triggers unnecessary client-side notifications for other users, and buries legitimate dialogue.

c. Utilizing reaction-spamming, repeated chaining of various media, to deface or obscure another user's message is treated with identical severity as text-spamming.

i.c.a. Rapid-Fire or Velocity Spamming

i.c.a.a. The high-frequency transmission of short, fragmented messages or characters in rapid succession. This tactic is engineered to hijack the visual field of a chat channel, trigger continuous audio/visual notifications for active users, and force legitimate conversation off the screen.

i.c.b. Block / Wall-of-Text Spamming
i.c.b.a. The deployment of massive, dense paragraphs of text, repeated copy-pastes ("copypastas"), or senseless character strings. This includes the utilization of hidden markdown or extensive vertical spacing designed to force users to scroll excessively to bypass the obstruction.

i.c.c. 3. Syntactic Formatting Abuse (Zalgo & Glitch Text)
i.c.c.a. The deployment of text corrupted with excessive combining diacritical marks (Zalgo text). This formatting intentionally breaks the standard graphical user interface (GUI) of the Discord client, bleeding text into adjacent messages and rendering the channel unreadable.


i.c.d. Mass-Mention / Ping Spamming
i.c.d.a. The malicious inclusion of multiple user tags, role identifiers, or specific administrative handles within a message. This is a severe infraction designed to force system-level push notifications onto users who are otherwise disengaged.

i.c.e. Ghost Pinging
i.c.e.a. The act of sending a message containing a user or role mention and immediately deleting it. This exploits a client-side vulnerability, leaving the targeted user with a persistent notification indicator but no visible source, causing systemic psychological irritation and confusion.

i.c.f. Reaction and Emoji Flooding
i.c.f.a. The systematic attachment of maximum allowable emojis or animated graphics to a single message or across multiple consecutive posts. This is often weaponized to deface administrative announcements, obscure user text, or artificially trigger client-side rendering lag on lower-end devices.

i.c.f.b. The user is permissible to allow the use a maximum of five( 5) emojis and/or reactions per message. Any other constituted messages that follow can be challenged for administrative action.

i.c.g. Media & GIF Saturation
i.c.g.a. The relentless posting of large image files, embedded videos, or flashing GIFs. This not only derails the thematic continuity of a channel but actively consumes the network bandwidth and memory caches of every constituent viewing the stream of text

i.c.h. Cross-Channel / Multi-Placement Spamming
i.c.h.a. The copy-pasting of a singular message, advertisement, or complaint across every available public text channel simultaneously, completely disregarding channel jurisdiction and boundaries.

i.c.i. Direct Message (DM) Scraping & Raiding
i.c.i.a Using a shared presence in a server to systematically harvest user lists and blast unauthorized advertisements, invitations, and promotions will result in penalties as per administrative action.

i.c.i.b. Phishing links, raids, or malicious invites directly into the private inboxes of community members accounts as a separate case and will be bannable without deliberation and further discussion.

i.c.j. Ticket / Support Line Exploitation
i.c.j.a. The repetitive opening of fraudulent administrative tickets or the flooding of support channels with redundant inquiries. This is a tactical maneuver designed to paralyze the Moderation Directorate by exhausting staff resources and delaying response times for legitimate cases.

    b. If a constituent creates an administrative ticket and purposefully delays its execution through idling, artificial unreadiness, or bad-faith stall tactics, the request will be summarily dissolved.

    c. The offender will be flagged in the ledger and may face localized communication restrictions for disrupting service efficiency.

  • vi.a.3. Proscription of Ineligible and Redundant Requests (Miscellaneous Tickets)
    a. The creation of tickets for game modes, milestones, or trials that the client has already successfully completed is strictly prohibited.

    b. The Carry Service system is engineered exclusively to provide critical assistance to first-time clearers and struggling constituents.

    c. Consequently, duplicated tickets, joke or troll requests, unauthorized connectivity testing, and any other uncoordinated administrative entries are entirely forbidden.

  • vi.a.4. Suppression of Personnel Harassment and Notification Flooding
    a. Clients must strictly refrain from harassing members of the Carry Team.

    b. Team operators manage a high volume of active tickets simultaneously and process requests systematically.

    c. Flooding public channels, ticket logs, or private direct messages with continuous notifications, demands for speed, or repetitive pings is recognized as a disruptive infraction; personnel will reach out to your query when your slot in the queue naturally opens.

  • vi.a.5. Absolute Compliance with Tactical Directives (Following Instructions)
    a. When entering a trial, the client must maintain absolute adherence to the tactical lead, strategy formulations, and mechanical instructions provided by the execution team.


    b. The operators possess supreme authority over the match strategy.

    c. If a client deliberately ignores directives, acts rogue during a run, or refuses to listen to mechanical callouts, the team is fully authorized to close the ticket immediately and leave the non-compliant user to their own devices.

  • vi.a.6. Rate-Limiting and Systemic Ticket Throttling (Spamming Tickets)
    a. To ensure every constituent receives a fair, equal opportunity to secure assistance, ticket generation is subjected to strict administrative throttling. Standard clients are limited to opening exactly one (1) assistance ticket every eight (8) hours.

    b. For specialized trial queues, this rate-limit is adjusted to one (1) ticket every two (2) hours.

    c. Attempting to bypass these cooldowns via script manipulation or rapid re-opening is treated as a minor systemic raid.

SUB-CLAUSE B: CARRY TEAM CODE OF CONDUCT AND DISCIPLINE

Carry Team Members are vetted operators entrusted with specialized roles and administrative permissions over tickets. Team status is not a license for elitism but rather it is a binding covenant of civic responsibility and professional execution.

  • vi.b.1. Mandated Professionalism and Client Preservation (Respecting the Client)
    a. Carry Team members must maintain an attitude of absolute, unyielding respect toward every client, regardless of the client’s mechanical skill level or past performance.

    b. You applied to this position for the explicit purpose of providing elite assistance to those who lack it.

    c. If an operator insults a client, mocks their mistakes, or exhibits toxic elitism, their specialized role will be instantly revoked, and their profile will face standard behavioral discipline under Clause III.

  • vi.b.2. Global Availability and Temporal Patience (Exercising Patience)
    a. Operators are required to maintain a high level of patience when managing the queue.

    b. The server’s populace is distributed globally across conflicting time zones, meaning clients will frequently be unavailable or sluggish to respond due to real-world latency or scheduling offsets.

    c. If an assigned client is temporarily offline, the operator must leave the ticket open and pivot to assist other active constituents in the queue rather than summarily dismissing the request.

  • vi.b.3. Strict Prohibition of Administrative Malfeasance (Abuse of Authority)
    a. Carry Team operators are expected to remain completely trustworthy with the specialized permissions, channel access, and ticket-management tools granted to them.

    b. Deliberately closing active tickets without a valid, documented reason, or adding unauthorized, uncoordinated third parties into private ticket chambers without administrative authorization is recognized as an act of bad-faith power abuse that will result in immediate removal from the staff directory.

  • vi.b.4. Confidentiality of Internal Operations (Data Isolation)
    a. All internal information, operational strategies, sensitive administration logs, and private conversations must remain completely confidential.

    b. Operators are strictly prohibited from discussing internal team data, sensitive client information, or relative staff applications within public ticket channels, unverified chat environments, or external peripheral applications.

    c. Operational security must be maintained across all vectors.

  • vi.b.5. Operational Activity and Availability Communication (Mandatory Activity)
    a. Maintaining an active, visible presence within the service sector is a core requirement of holding a team position.

    b. If real-world obligations, academic schedules, or personal circumstances require an operator to enter a period of prolonged inactivity, they are obligated to formally notify the Administration team beforehand.

    c. Unannounced disappearances or persistent stagnation will result in the quiet decommissioning of your operator status due to inactivity.
